I've been reflecting on my job search lately, and how many roles place intense focus on specific tools or platforms, often at the expense of broader security expertise.

As someone with a strong background in security domains, it's been frustrating to be overlooked (or ghosted) simply because I don't have 2+ years of hands-on experience with *Tool X* or *Platform Y*. Especially when most tools can be learned in a matter of days or weeks.

What's much harder to teach is how to think about security **holistically: threat modeling, risk analysis, vulnerability management, architecture decisions, and the attacker mindset.

Security is a mindset, not a checklist. And it's that mindset that keeps organizations resilient long after a specific tool goes out of fashion.

I don't say this to complain, but to spark a conversation.

What do we lean so heavily on tool checklists when hiring cybersecurity roles?

Are organizations missing out on talented security professionals who may not have the "right" tool listed on their resume?

If you're hiring, or know someone who is, I'd love to connect. I bring deep security knowledge, a fast learning curve when it comes to tools, and a genuine passion for keeping enterprises protected.

I'd also love to hear your thoughts, whether you agree or disagree.
