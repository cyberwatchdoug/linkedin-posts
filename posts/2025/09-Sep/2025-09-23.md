One of those small but mighty tricks that can save hours of guesswork: test containers in Kubernetes.

My number one use case is simple: testing connectivity between pods on different nodes.

Networking issues are sneaky. Instead of digging endlessly through configs and logs, I’ve found it’s often faster to just drop a test container into the cluster and see what works and what doesn’t.

In Kubernetes, so much of what “breaks” isn’t about your code.

It’s about the environment it runs in. 

Test containers let you peel back that layer instantly, without needing to push new code and hope for the best.

It’s my go-to move when I’m deep in break/fix/rebuild sessions in my homelab.

Next time you’re troubleshooting, try spinning up a disposable test container. It might just be the shortcut you didn’t know you needed.
