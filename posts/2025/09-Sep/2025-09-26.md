Every engineer hits a crossroads: double down on what works, or pivot to what might. For me, it was staring at my own "imperative" self-hosted containerized workflow, realizing I'd created too much *technical debt*.

The decision? Stick with my siloed, manual approach **or** embrace a new challenge of complexity with Kubernetes. Continue with instant gratification and endless technical debt **or** build something declarative with GitOps workflows.

The variables were not just technical. It's about *sustainability* when you are the single point of failure. It was the long-term viability of running everything myself and facing down the reality that every fix added more hidden liabilities. I started asking: *Who's auditing my change history or strategies when it's just me on the hot seat?*

Shifting toward a declarative, GitOps-driven workflow felt like betting on the upfront complexity in exchange for resilience that won't evaporate the moment I step away.

My pivot isn't finished. There's significant work ahead to be shared transparently; not in isolation. The lessons aren't just technical to me; they're personal, uncomfortable at times, and oddly validating. I'm drawn to future-proofing through automation, not just for convenience but for survival.

Anyone else betting their technical debt against tomorrow's operational sanity?
